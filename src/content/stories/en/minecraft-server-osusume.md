Public Minecraft servers range from places for relaxed building and survival to communities centered on economies, quests, and events. Comparing the edition you use, what you want to play, and the guidance available before joining makes it easier to find a server you can enjoy for a long time.

This article is edited by the official Aceserver portal. It first introduces other public servers using their official information, then presents Aceserver as one of the options at the end.

## Editorial policy

The order below is not a ranking of popularity, size, safety, or quality. On August 1, 2026, we selected servers whose official sites showed a joining route or connection information, supported editions, style of play, and an entry point for rules or guidance. They are arranged to make their different styles easier to compare.

Server availability, supported versions, age requirements, rules, and events can change. Always open each server's official guidance and check the latest information before joining.

## Three things to check before comparing

1. **Whether you use Java or Bedrock**: If you will play with friends, confirm that everyone can join.
2. **Whether the play style fits**: Decide whether you want mostly vanilla-style life, building, an economy, quests, events, or something else.
3. **Whether you can read the rules and joining guide**: A server with clear steps, prohibited behavior, and a place to ask questions is easier for a first visit.

## Six recommended Minecraft servers

> **About the images**
>
> Each image is an original illustration made to make the play style described below easier to understand. It is not a screenshot of the actual server or an official distributed image.

### 1. [Nishisaba](https://nishisaba.jp/)

![Original illustration of travellers with a map walking through a block-built riverside town with homes, fields, and a bridge](/uploads/stories/minecraft-server-osusume-nishisaba.webp)

Nishisaba is a public cross-play server for Java and Bedrock that presents three worlds: main, survival, and lobby. It can suit players who want freeform building and adventure while also using additions such as mcMMO and home settings. Its official site brings together rules, joining information, Discord, and a wiki.

### 2. [Manmamiya](https://www.manmamiya.work/)

![Original illustration of a block-built community with homes, gardens, and a small boat carrying supplies](/uploads/stories/minecraft-server-osusume-manmamiya.webp)

Manmamiya accepts both Java and Bedrock players and keeps a vanilla feel while offering useful multiplayer features such as land protection, a resource world, and warps. Players interested in sightseeing, building, events, and long-running survival play can get a clear picture by reading its official site and wiki first. Check its official age guidance before joining as well.

### 3. [Tsubusaba](https://tsubu-server.github.io/)

![Original illustration of block-styled players building contraptions and trading in a colorful biome](/uploads/stories/minecraft-server-osusume-tsubusaba.webp)

Tsubusaba supports Java and Bedrock as a lifestyle-oriented server. Alongside a main world with unusual biomes, it introduces a resource world and a trap-focused server for building contraptions. It is a candidate for people who like gradually developing a life through land protection, jobs, and player shops.

### 4. [Munika Server](https://munikasvr.net/)

![Original illustration of block-styled adventurers at a quest hub and market at dusk](/uploads/stories/minecraft-server-osusume-munika.webp)

Munika Server accepts both Java and Bedrock players and adds economy, skills, and quest elements to survival play. It can suit people who want goals and events in addition to ordinary survival. Its official site provides entry points for joining, rules, support, and Discord.

### 5. [Noasaba](https://noasaba.com/)

![Original illustration of block-styled players fishing and tending a small riverside survival base](/uploads/stories/minecraft-server-osusume-noasaba.webp)

Noasaba is an independently run server for Java and Bedrock that presents itself as vanilla survival. Consider it if you want a multiplayer presence while staying close to everyday Minecraft. Its official site links to connection information, rules, usage notes, a map, and Discord.

### 6. Aceserver

![Original illustration of block-styled players around a map in a freeform building area at sunset](/uploads/stories/minecraft-server-osusume-aceserver.webp)

Aceserver is a free public Minecraft server that anyone can join from either Java or Bedrock. It may suit people who want near-vanilla, highly free play while using Discord as the entry point for joining guidance and community information.

Before joining, check the [Aceserver portal](/en/), [official Discord](https://discord.gg/acsv), and [official WIKI](https://asv-wiki.acecore.net) in that order. You can then decide whether it fits your preferred way to play before joining.

## If you are unsure, compare the official guides

Do not decide only because a server is popular or well known. Compare each server's joining steps, rules, and world descriptions. After checking Java/Bedrock support, whether your friends can join, and whether the play style fits, try the one that interests you most.
