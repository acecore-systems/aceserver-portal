At the beginning of Minecraft Bedrock, it can be hard to know what to build first. You do not need an elaborate house immediately. A setup that gets you safely through the night and keeps tools and food available makes later exploration and building much easier.

This list assumes a new survival world for one person or a small group. Terrain and play style matter, so choose what you need first.

## Six priorities for the first day

### 1. Crafting table

Once you have wood, make a crafting table. It is the center for making the tools, torches, and bed you need next. Putting one down early speeds up every later step.

### 2. Wooden and stone tools

Use wooden tools to gather stone, then make a stone pickaxe, axe, and sword. Instead of upgrading everything at once, make the tools that keep mining and staying safe moving forward.

### 3. Furnace

A furnace cooks food and smelts ore. Make one as soon as you have stone, and prepare a little food before setting out to explore.

### 4. Torches

Torches provide light for nights and caves. Place them around your base, at entrances, and along cave routes. Pick one side to place them on while exploring to help mark the way back.

### 5. Bed

Once you find a safe place, make a bed early. It helps you get through the night and set a respawn point. Check that the surroundings are safe before placing it.

### 6. Temporary base

Your first house can be small. A roof, entrance, light, bed, crafting table, furnace, and chest are enough to start the next goal. Save large-scale decoration for after daily survival is stable.

## Six builds for a stable routine

### 7. Chests and simple sorting

Keeping materials, food, and tools in one place makes necessary items harder to find. At first, broad groups such as ore, building blocks, and food plus tools are enough.

### 8. A food source

Keep food from running out during exploration by making room near the base for crops or for animals you find. Combining a farm with food gathered while exploring is easier to sustain than relying on only one source.

### 9. Small farm

Make a small farm near water so you can harvest and replant. Leave large automatic systems for later and first aim for enough food to avoid running out.

### 10. Water and a safe route down

Water helps with farms and with moving through uneven terrain. At cliffs and cave entrances, add ladders, stairs, and light so you always leave a way back.

### 11. Iron tools and armor

When you find iron, prioritize a pickaxe and protection for risky exploration. Stabilizing mining and returning home reduces the work lost when something goes wrong.

### 12. Markers and exploration routes

Before going far from your base, mark paths and use maps or signs so you can return. In multiplayer, this also makes shared routes and locations easier for everyone to understand.

## Priorities by situation

- If night is near, prioritize a bed, light, and a temporary base.
- If you find a cave, prioritize torches, food, and markers for the way back.
- If you find iron, prioritize a furnace, pickaxe, and protection.
- Once daily survival is stable, move on to farms, storage, and material gathering for building.

This is not a list with one correct answer. Choose in the order that helps you get through tonight, return from the next exploration trip, and keep food available, and you can grow a base on almost any terrain.
